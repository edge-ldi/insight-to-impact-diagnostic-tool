Why learning fails

The gap between effort
and impact is real.

The L&D industry spends billions designing and delivering learning. But the evidence is stark: most of it doesn't translate into the performance change it was meant to create.

70%
of learning fails to transfer to the job
Axonify, 2022
15%
of what's learned actually gets applied on the job
Harvard Business Review, 2019
The transfer problem is structural, not personal.
It's not that people can't learn. It's that learning initiatives are routinely designed without a clear problem statement, delivered into the wrong environment, and measured too late — if at all.
🎯
Wrong problem, wrong solution
Without rigorous discovery, L&D teams build solutions to the symptoms they're told about — not the root causes that are actually driving the gap.
🏗️
Design without context
Learning built without a deep understanding of the learner's real environment, constraints, and motivations rarely lands as intended.
📊
Measurement as afterthought
Evaluation is typically bolted on at the end — making it impossible to prove value, identify what's working, or make confident decisions about future investment.
⚙️
Delivery ecosystems left unprepared
Even excellent learning design fails when facilitators aren't ready, technology isn't tested, and managers haven't been activated to support transfer.
